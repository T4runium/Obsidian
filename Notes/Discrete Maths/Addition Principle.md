If $A$ and $B$ are 2 finite and [[Set Theory#^019723|disjoint sets]] then:
$$
|A \cup B| = |A| + |B|
$$
In general, for $A_{1}A_{2}\dots A_{n}$ disjoint pairs:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|A_{i}| = |A_{1}\cup A_{2}\dots\cup A_{n}|  
$$

Use it for an **"or"** between mutually exclusive cases. For successive choices ("and then") use the [[Multiplication Principle]]

# Complement counting
If $B \subseteq A$ then $B$ and $A \setminus B$ are disjoint, so
$$
|A \setminus B| = |A| - |B|
$$
"At least one" = everything minus "none"
e.g. 4-letter words with at least one E: $26^4 - 25^4$

Sets not disjoint? Use the [[Inclusion-Exclusion Principle]]
